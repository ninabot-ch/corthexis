A correction that is only applied is spent. A correction that is written down is
capital.

**Why:** the expensive part of working with an agent is not the task, it is
re-explaining the same constraint every session — house conventions, which
approach was already rejected and why, what "done" means here. That knowledge
exists only in the human's head until something writes it down. Once it is a
note, the cost of it is paid once.

**How to apply:** when a human corrects you, do not just comply. Write a
`feedback` note carrying three things:

1. the rule, stated as an instruction, not as a story about the incident;
2. **Why:** the reason — a rule without its reason gets misapplied the moment
   circumstances shift slightly;
3. **How to apply:** the trigger. When should a future session recall this?

Notes typed `feedback` or `user` are pinned to the top tier of the generated
index, above the recency window, because these are the rules that must be read
without anyone knowing to look for them. That placement is the whole point:
searchable knowledge is good, but a rule you have to know to search for has
already failed.

Keep them short. A rule that takes three paragraphs to state is two rules.
